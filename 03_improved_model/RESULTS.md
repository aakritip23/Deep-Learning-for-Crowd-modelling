# Results - which model should we develop?  (CPU comparison, 2026-11-11)

**Decision: the Social Transformer** (`trajpred/models.py`, 0.65 M parameters): Transformer encoder over the 8 observed steps +
cross-attention over the nearest <=16 neighbours + a decoder that predicts residuals on top of a constant-velocity
extrapolation, trained directly on ADE + 0.5 FDE with mirror / scale / noise augmentation.

Metrics are the TrajNet ones (metres, lower is better): ADE (= MAD), FDE (= FAD), **Avg = (ADE + FDE) / 2** (the ranking score;
the professor's published Social LSTM: Avg 1.3865 = (2.098 + 0.675) / 2 on the hidden-label test server).

## 1. How the existing model was re-scored
The reference repository ships no trained weights. A local smoke test of its training script on a laptop CPU (log not included here) averaged
about 1.5 s per batch (4,561 batches per epoch, i.e. roughly 2 hours per epoch; up to 5-7 s per batch late in a second run) and the first epoch ran out of
memory near its end, so re-training it for many epochs was impractical. Instead **Social LSTM and Vanilla LSTM were re-implemented inside our harness**
(`trajpred/reference_lstm.py`: Alahi et al. architecture, the professor's configuration - rnn 128, embedding 64, 4x4 grid, dropout 0.5,
RMSprop 3e-3, bivariate-Gaussian NLL, teacher forcing, positions relative to the first observed point) and trained/scored on the same data,
splits and metrics as the Transformers. Sanity check: on unseen videos the re-implementation lands at **1.57x** the constant-velocity
score; the professor's published number is **1.55x** the TrajNet linear baseline (1.3865 / 0.894) - the reproduction is consistent.

Two evaluation protocols (the test split has hidden labels, so it cannot be scored locally):
* **official** - train -> validation (11,403 trajectories). The validation files are rotated, noisy copies of training files (the dataset's own augmentation: same frames and
  pedestrian ids, rotation by a multiple of 30 degrees, Gaussian noise of about 0.1), so this protocol is in-sample and optimistic.
* **held-out videos** - 7 whole videos (`biwi_hotel, crowds_zara02, students003, PETS09-S2L1, bookstore_3, hyang_5, gates_3`; 7,425 rows)
  never seen in training; closest to the real test (unseen videos). **Trust this column more.** Each held-out trajectory is present three times (original + two augmented copies),
  i.e. 2,475 unique trajectories; the mean errors are unaffected by the repetition, but confidence intervals must resample the unique trajectories (Section 3).

## 3. Is the gap real?  (paired bootstrap over trajectories, B - A, negative = B better, 95% CI)
| A -> B | official | held-out videos |
|---|---|---|
| Individual TF -> **Social TF** | -0.046 [-0.053, -0.039] | -0.024 [-0.033, -0.016] |
| Refined Social LSTM -> **Social TF** | -0.097 [-0.107, -0.086] | -0.035 [-0.045, -0.026] |
| Refined Vanilla LSTM -> **Social TF** | -0.093 [-0.104, -0.083] | -0.032 [-0.042, -0.023] |
| Individual TF -> individual TF + source emb. | -0.008 [-0.011, -0.005] | (single run: 1.044 -> 1.037) |

Seed-to-seed noise (3 seeds of the individual TF, official): Avg 1.0545 / 1.0524 / 1.0517, **std 0.0014** - the differences above are far larger.
Not significant on the small sources (mot, 107 / 321 trajectories) in several comparisons.

**Correction for the held-out column (added when the final report was written).** The intervals above resample rows, but the held-out set contains every trajectory three times
(original + two augmented copies), so its rows are not independent and the intervals are too narrow. Resampling the 2,475 unique trajectories instead (`compare_models.py --cluster`)
gives, for the 30-epoch models: Individual TF -> Social TF **-0.024 [-0.038, -0.011]**, refined Social LSTM -> Social TF **-0.035 [-0.049, -0.022]**, refined Vanilla LSTM -> Social TF
**-0.032 [-0.046, -0.019]**. The intervals are about 1.3-1.5 times wider, none includes zero, so the conclusions stand. The official-split intervals are not affected by the repetition
(each validation trajectory occurs once) but that split is in-sample.

## 4. What the evidence says
1. **The existing formulation is fragile.** Predicting absolute positions with teacher forcing makes the rollout error compound: validation Avg is best at epoch
   2-3 and then explodes (4.7+) while the training loss keeps improving. Even at its best epoch it is worse than constant velocity (1.82x / 1.57x).
2. **Refining it helps a lot, but only part of the way.** Predicting per-step velocities (+ Adam, dropout 0.1) fixes the instability: 1.10 / 1.05, i.e. better than
   constant velocity (0.90-0.92x). In this family the social-pooling grid gives **no gain** (Vanilla 1.102 vs Social 1.105 official; 1.051 vs 1.055 held-out).
3. **A Transformer encoder is better still** (0.87-0.89x), and **neighbour attention adds a further clear gain** (0.84 / 0.87x) - on biwi, crowds and Stanford
   (not significant on the tiny mot source). Overall: Social TF is **54 % / 44 % lower than the professor-configured Social LSTM** (official / held-out) and
   **8.5 % / 3.0 % lower than the best refined LSTM**.
4. Source-dataset embedding: small gain for the individual model (it carries information neighbours would otherwise provide), **no gain for the Social Transformer** (1.010 vs 1.008) -> not used (`--source-emb` stays as an option).
5. Cheap extras: flip TTA -0.004 (1.008 -> 1.004 official; 1.020 -> 1.016 held-out); a 3-seed ensemble about -0.005 (individual TF); mixing in weaker models *hurts* (1.004 -> 1.024),
   so ensemble only seeds of the same social model (what `run_lab.sh` does).
6. Not pursued: multimodal / diffusion / Mamba models - TrajNet scores one prediction per pedestrian, so best-of-K generative models would not improve the metric (see `../05_notes/research_notes.md`).

## 5. Rough projection to the TrajNet test-server scale (illustration only)
If the ratio to a simple linear baseline transfers (the published linear baseline scores 0.894 there; our individual Transformer sits at 0.87-0.89x of constant velocity, the
published Transformer at 0.87x of that baseline - consistent), the Social Transformer would score about **0.75 (official ratio) - 0.78 (held-out ratio)**, versus the
professor's 1.3865 (~44-46 % lower) and the best published entries around 0.77-0.78. **This is an extrapolation, not a measurement;** the only real test is a submission
to the TrajNet server (the lab pipeline writes the submission files).

## 6. What to expect in the lab (health check)
Social TF, 30 epochs, seed 0: official Avg ~1.00-1.01, held-out ~1.02, individual TF ~1.05 / 1.04, constant velocity 1.206 / 1.169; seed std ~0.0015. If the lab numbers are far from these,
something differs (data path, library version, a bug) - compare per-source tables first. The seed ensemble (5 final-fit seeds) and the final fit on train+validation (50 % more data) are expected to
help slightly on unseen videos; this cannot be measured locally.

## 7. Limitations / not verified
* The 30-epoch study of Sections 2-4 was run on CPU. The same code was then run end to end on a Colab GPU in `quick` mode (8 epochs, Section 10), which reproduces the CPU numbers within seed noise.
  The full-length lab pipeline (`run_lab.sh`: 3-seed ensembles, final fit on train + validation) has been dry-run in smoke mode only.
* Single run per model in the table (seed noise measured only for the individual TF); one held-out split of 7 videos (2,475 unique trajectories, see Section 1 and the correction in Section 3).
* The LSTM family was lightly tuned (one extra configuration + the velocity refinement); a heavily tuned LSTM could narrow the 3 % held-out gap.
* The re-implemented Social LSTM is not the professor's code; its social neighbourhood was set to 4 m (the professor's grid size is derived from dataset pixel sizes; I did not verify its units).
* Test-set ground truth is hidden: no local test score exists.
* Scenes mostly contain one pedestrian (median 1), so the social module can only help on the crowded subset.

## 9. Short schedules (Colab notebook `colab_social_transformer.ipynb`)
How good is the Social Transformer with fewer epochs?  Each run uses its own full cosine schedule (CPU, seed 0, official split unless noted; Avg, lower is better):

| Run | Avg | vs constant velocity |
|---|---|---|
| Constant velocity | 1.206 (held-out 1.169) | 1.00x |
| Social Transformer, 4 epochs | 1.066 | 0.88x |
| Social Transformer, **8 epochs (quick mode)** | **1.044** (held-out 1.027) | 0.87x |
| Social Transformer, 30 epochs (full study) | 1.008 (held-out 1.020) | 0.84x |
| Individual Transformer, 8 epochs | 1.075 (held-out 1.055) | 0.89x |
| Social LSTM refined (velocity), 8 epochs | 1.127 | 0.93x |
| Social LSTM, professor's settings, 4 epochs | 2.006 (best epoch; unstable) | 1.66x |

Even 4 epochs beat constant velocity by 12 %; 8 epochs already beat the fully trained individual Transformer (1.054) and the refined LSTM (1.105); on held-out videos
8 epochs are within 0.007 of the 30-epoch result.  `colab_run.py --mode quick` uses these epoch counts.

* The GPU numbers agree with the CPU runs of the same schedule (Section 9: 1.044 / 1.027 for the Social Transformer) within seed noise, so the pipeline is device-independent as intended.
* Training time on the Colab GPU: about 5-8 s per Transformer epoch and 26 s per LSTM epoch; all six trainings together took 8.8 minutes (a CPU needs 2-4 minutes per Transformer epoch).
* The professor-settings LSTM is again unstable: validation Avg 2.35 / **1.85** / 2.18 / 2.26 over its 4 epochs (best epoch 2 is kept), as described in Section 4.
* The submission in `04_results/colab_final_run/submission.zip` (20 files, 37,776 predicted rows, accepted by `check_submission.py`) comes from the official-split Social Transformer of this run with flip TTA.
  Ground truth of the test split is hidden, so **no test-server score exists yet**; the 30-epoch / final-fit models of Sections 2-6 would be expected to score slightly better.
